Android · India

Your spending, worked out on your phone.

Ledger reads the transaction alerts your bank already sends you and turns them into a spending record. No account. No server. No ads. Nothing to sign up for.

Download the APK Privacy policy

What it does

  • Reads your bank SMS, on the device. Every debit, credit, card payment and UPI transfer your bank texts you becomes a row, categorised automatically.
  • Shows where the money went. This month, trends over time, spending by category, and the merchants you actually spend at.
  • Tells you which card to use. Give it the amount and the merchant and it names the card that pays most, working from your own spending rather than a brochure.
  • Tracks what you own. Enter your holdings and it works out net worth, how it is split across asset classes, and how long your cash would last.
  • Learns. Tag a payee once and future messages from it categorise themselves.

What it does not do

There is no account, so there is nothing to sign up for and nothing to be locked out of. There is no server, so your spending is not sitting on someone else's computer. There are no ads and no analytics, so nothing about you is worth collecting in the first place.

The app can optionally sync to companion software on your own computer, on your own wifi, if you want a fuller history there. That is off unless you set it up, and the data goes to your machine — never to us. The privacy policy says exactly what moves and when.

Why SMS

On an Indian phone, the bank's transaction alert is the only machine-readable record that a payment happened. Reading it is what lets an expense tracker be accurate without you typing in every purchase. Ledger matches those messages against a table of bank formats on the device; anything that is not a transaction is discarded.

The format table updates daily over the internet so a bank that changes its wording keeps working. That update is a download — nothing about you is sent with it.
