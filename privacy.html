Ledger

Privacy Policy

The Ledger app has no account and no sign-up: it reads your bank SMS on your phone and sends them nowhere. If someone gives you a link to a hosted Ledger, that is a separate thing, and this page says plainly what it does.

Last updated 11 September 2026.

Ledger is a personal expense tracker. It reads the transaction SMS your bank already sends you, turns them into a spending record, and shows you where your money went. Everything it works out, it works out on your phone.

What Ledger reads

Your SMS messages

This is the app's core function. A bank transaction alert — "Rs 450 spent on card XX1234 at …" — is the only record of a payment that exists on the phone, so reading it is the only way to build a spending history without you typing in every purchase.

  • Messages are read on the device and matched against a table of bank message formats.
  • A message that matches becomes a transaction: date, amount, direction, account last-four, merchant, category.
  • A message that does not match is discarded, with the one exception described under “Reporting a message format”.
  • Ledger never reads your messages for any other purpose, never builds a contact graph from them, and never transmits your message history to us or to anyone else.

Nothing else

Ledger does not request contacts, location, camera, microphone, call logs, photos or files. It contains no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK and no crash-reporting SDK.

Where your data lives

In the app's private storage on your phone, and nowhere else by default.

Uninstalling Ledger deletes all of it. Android backup is disabled for this app, so it is not copied into your Google account backup either.

What leaves your phone

Three things, and only three.

1. Rule updates — a download, never an upload

Once a day Ledger fetches a file of bank message formats over HTTPS from GitHub, so that a bank which changes its wording is understood without waiting for an app update. It is a plain file download. Nothing about you is sent with it. GitHub, as the host, sees the network request in the ordinary way any website sees a visitor.

2. Sync to a computer you control — only if you set it up

Ledger can pair with companion software running on a machine you control — a computer on your own wifi, or a server you rent and administer yourself. If, and only if, you pair it:

  • Ledger sends your bank SMS to that machine, at an address you typed in yourself.
  • It is your computer. It is not our server, and we never see the data.
  • On your own network the connection is plain HTTP, because no certificate authority issues certificates for 192.168.x.x and the traffic never leaves your wifi. To any address outside it, Ledger uses HTTPS and will not send your messages in the clear.
  • An unpaired install — which is every install until you deliberately pair one — sends nothing anywhere.

3. A message format you choose to report

If Ledger sees a message that looks financial but matches no known format, it can offer to let you report it so the format can be added. This is never automatic. You tap to share, you see exactly what will be sent, and before it is shown to you every run of two or more digits is replaced with x — so amounts, dates, account numbers and reference numbers are destroyed, and only the shape of the sentence survives. You then choose where to send it with your phone's own share sheet.

What we collect about you

From the app: nothing. It has no account, we operate no server it talks to, and we cannot identify you. There is no database of app users because there is no such database.

If you were given a hosted Ledger

Someone can run this software on a machine they control and give you an account on it. That is not us, and it is a different privacy question — the person running that machine holds your data and is responsible for it. On such an instance:

  • You create a username and password. The password is stored hashed (scrypt), never in the clear.
  • Your ledger is separate from every other account on that machine — its own data, its own rebuilds, its own sync token. No account can read another's, and that separation is enforced on the server rather than in the page.
  • Your bank SMS, statements and everything derived from them sit on that person's machine. Ask them who can reach it and what their backups look like; those are their answers to give, not ours.
  • You can delete it yourself. Profile → Close this account removes your ledger and your sign-in together — the parsed transactions, the statements and SMS you uploaded, your categorisations and the username. It asks for your password rather than accepting the session you are already signed in with, and there is no soft delete and no grace period: the files are removed from that machine's disk. If a rebuild happens to be reading your data at that moment it will ask you to try again in a few minutes.
  • What that button cannot speak for is the machine's backups. If the person running it takes snapshots, a copy may survive in one until it ages out. That is their answer to give, not ours — ask them.

If you would rather nobody hold your data at all, install the app instead: it needs no account and no server, and this page's first section describes it.

Who we share it with

No one. Your data is not sold, rented, brokered or shared, because we do not have it.

The app lock, honestly

Ledger can require a PIN, password or fingerprint before it opens. The secret is stored as a PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hash with a random salt, so the PIN itself is not written down anywhere.

Be clear about what that protects: it stops a person holding your unlocked phone from opening the app. It is not disk encryption, and not a defence against someone who takes the device apart or has root access — on such a phone the app's files can be read regardless. Android's own device encryption and screen lock are what protect you there, and Ledger's lock sits on top of them rather than replacing them.

Children

Ledger is not directed at children and we do not knowingly process children's data.

Your rights

Because your data never leaves your device, you exercise every right directly and immediately: view it in the app, change or delete any entry, or uninstall to erase all of it at once. There is no request to make of us and no account to close. If you have paired a computer, delete the files there too — they are on your machine, under your control. On a hosted instance, Close this account under Profile does it for you.
